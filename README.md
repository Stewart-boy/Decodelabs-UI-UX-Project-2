🎯 DecodeLabs UI/UX Project 2 — E-Commerce Homepage Wireframe

A low-fidelity wireframe for a full e-commerce homepage, focused on establishing a clean, conversion-oriented information architecture before any visual design was applied.

This project was completed as Project 2 during my DecodeLabs UI/UX Design Internship Program, with a focus on layout structure, content hierarchy, and UX flow for an online storefront.

📌 Project Overview

An e-commerce brand needed a homepage structure capable of guiding a visitor from first impression through to purchase — covering navigation, promotions, product discovery, trust-building, and conversion, all before a single visual design decision was made. The goal was to map every section's purpose and placement at low fidelity, so that structure and usability could be validated independently of styling.

🎯 Target Audience

Online shoppers browsing a general retail storefront (apparel, footwear, accessories, home & living) who need to quickly understand what's on offer, trust the brand enough to buy, and find a clear path to checkout.

🧩 What's Inside

🧱 Full-Page Wireframe, including:

	•	Utility bar — shipping policy & support contact
	•	Header — logo, primary nav (Shop, Categories, About Us, Contact), search, account, cart
	•	Hero section — headline, supporting copy, dual CTA ("Shop Now" / "Learn More"), trust line (Free Shipping / 30-Day Returns / Secure Checkout), and a featured-product callout
	•	Value proposition strip — four short trust/benefit tiles
	•	Shop by Category — four-item category grid with item counts
	•	Featured Products — filterable product grid (All / Trending / New Arrivals / Best Sellers) with ratings and "Add to Cart"
	•	Why Shop With Us — a direct comparison block (Option Alpha vs. Option Beta) framing the brand against a generic alternative
	•	Customer Reviews — three testimonials with ratings and an aggregate score
	•	Newsletter signup — email capture with a no-spam reassurance line
	•	Footer — Company / Shop / Support / Legal link groups

🔑 Key Objective

Establish a homepage structure that answers four questions in order, with no ambiguity: What is this store? What can I buy? Why should I trust it? How do I take the next step?

💡 Key UX Insight

Trust signals are most effective when distributed throughout the page rather than concentrated in one place — shipping/returns appear in the utility bar, the hero, and the footer; reviews and guarantees reappear just before the newsletter CTA. This repetition addresses hesitation at the specific moments a shopper is likely to feel it, rather than front-loading all reassurance before any product is even seen.

🎨 Design Consideration

The wireframe was built to keep future visual design unconstrained while locking in:

	•	A consistent grid and spacing system across all sections
	•	Clear content hierarchy (headline → support copy → action) repeated section to section
	•	Component structure (cards, grids, CTAs) generic enough to carry any brand's visual identity
	•	Logical scroll order from awareness → consideration → trust → conversion

🛠️ Tool Used

Figma — low-fidelity wireframe layout, component structure, and export.

🧠 UX Process

	1.	Identified the core jobs a homepage needs to do for an e-commerce visitor.
	2.	Mapped those jobs to a section-by-section page structure.
	3.	Built each section in low fidelity using placeholder content, prioritizing hierarchy and flow over visual style.
	4.	Checked the structure against common e-commerce UX patterns (trust signals, product discovery, social proof, conversion paths).
	5.	Validated that every major CTA had a clear, singular purpose.
	6.	Used this wireframe as the structural foundation for a subsequent high-fidelity visual design pass.

🎓 Learning Outcomes

Through this project, I practiced:

	•	Information architecture for e-commerce
	•	Content hierarchy and visual flow at low fidelity
	•	Designing reusable, brand-agnostic component structures
	•	Placing trust signals at the moments they're needed most
	•	Structuring multi-section long-scroll layouts
	•	Preparing a wireframe as a foundation for high-fidelity design
	•	Figma layout and documentation for a public portfolio
